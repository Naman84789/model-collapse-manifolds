\noindent\textbf{Claim.} For data in a $\sigma$-tube about a manifold, any reverse-SDE sampler
whose learned normal score slope is bounded by $\kb$ retains normal variance at least
$1/(8\kb^2)$ as $\sigma\to0$, for \emph{every} truncation time and \emph{every} schedule. So a
slope-capped model cannot track a thinning manifold, and bounded relative error forces
$\kb\gtrsim1/\sigma$, i.e.\ a slope budget growing like $\sqrt J$ in the Fisher information. This bounds the
network's normal Lipschitz norm, not its parameter count: a fixed architecture attains any
$\kb$ by enlarging its weights. This note
is self-contained; \S\ref{sec:U} is the entire argument (comparison principle, integrating
factor, one Gamma integral).

\section{Setup}
Let $M\subset\R^d$ be a compact smooth $k$-manifold with reach $\tau>0$ and
$X_0=\varphi(U)+\sigma N_\perp$, $U$ uniform on $M$, $N_\perp$ standard normal in the normal
space. Use the variance-preserving OU process $X_t=aX_0+sZ$, $a=e^{-t/2}$, $s^2=1-e^{-t}$.
Work in one normal coordinate $x\in\R$, treating the fiber marginal as Gaussian and decoupled
from tangential position (\emph{flat-tube idealization}; curvature enters at $O(s^2/\tau)$).
Then $X_0\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigsq)$, $X_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\gamma^2)$ with
$\gamma^2=a^2\sigsq+s^2$, and the denoising target is exactly linear:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^*(x,t)=\kappa(t)x,\qquad \kappa(t)=s(t)/\gamma^2(t).
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
Maximising \eqref{eq:kappa} over $u:=s^2$ gives $u^\star=\sigsq/(1-\sigsq)$ and
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\max}=\tfrac{1}{2\sigma\sqrt{1-\sigsq}}=\tfrac{1}{2\sigma}(1+O(\sigsq)).
\label{eq:peak}
\end{equation}
\emph{This is the mechanism:} $\kappa$ rises from $0$, peaks at $\approx1/(2\sigma)$ near
$t\approx\sigsq$, and returns to $0$ as $t\to0$. The score is steep only inside a short, finite
window of noise levels, and the peak diverges as the tube thins.

Let $X_t$ be the sampler's \emph{own} ensemble at noise level $t$ and define the realised
normal slope
\[
\hat\kappa(t):=\mathrm{Cov}\big(X_t,\hat\varepsilon(X_t,t)\big)\big/\mathrm{Var}(X_t),
\]
the $L^2$ regression slope of the estimator under the law the sampler actually visits. No
decomposition of the network is needed. The only hypothesis used below is
\begin{equation}
\hat\kappa(t)\le\kb<\infty\quad\text{for all }t,
\label{eq:cap}
\end{equation}
a finite normal Lipschitz constant. This bounds the network's normal \emph{slope}, a
weight-norm quantity, not its parameter count: a fixed architecture realises arbitrarily large
$\kb$ by scaling its weights, so nothing below lower-bounds network size. Put
$\rho:=2\sigma\kb=\kb/\kappa_{\max}+O(\sigsq)$, the single dimensionless knob: how close the
network's ceiling gets to the peak slope the data demands.

\section{Two structural facts}
\begin{lemma}[variance ODE]\label{lem:ode}
Under the reverse Euler scheme $x\!\leftarrow\!x+(-\tfrac12x+\hat\varepsilon/s)dt+\sqrt{|dt|}\,\xi$
with affine estimator, the ensemble normal variance obeys, in the small-step limit,
\begin{equation}
-\frac{dV}{dt}=\Big(1-\frac{2\hat\kappa(t)}{s(t)}\Big)V+1 .
\label{eq:varode}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\noindent\emph{Check:} with the exact slope $\hat\kappa=\kappa$, $V(t)=\gamma^2(t)$ solves
\eqref{eq:varode} identically. The forcing $+1$ is the sampler's own injected noise.

\begin{lemma}[Lipschitz sufficiency]\label{lem:shield}
If $\hat\varepsilon(\cdot,t)$ is $\kb$-Lipschitz in the normal coordinate then
$\hat\kappa(t)\le\kb$ under any law: for an i.i.d.\ copy $X'$ of $X$,
$\mathrm{Cov}(X,g(X))=\tfrac12\E[(X-X')(g(X)-g(X'))]\le\tfrac{\kb}{2}\E[(X-X')^2]
=\kb\,\mathrm{Var}(X)$. Lipschitz control is sufficient, not necessary.
\end{lemma}
\noindent This is why the floor is not an artifact of linearising the score. \eqref{eq:varode}
is an \emph{identity} for an arbitrary nonlinear $\hat\varepsilon$, since one Euler step enters
the variance only through $\mathrm{Cov}(X,\hat\varepsilon(X))$; the nonlinearity is not
discarded, it is absorbed into the definition of $\hat\kappa$.

\paragraph{Singular rescaling.} Put $t=\sigsq u$, $V=\sigsq W$, $\hat\kappa=(1/\sigma)m(u)$.
Since $s=\sqrt t(1+O(t))$, \eqref{eq:varode} becomes, as $\sigma\to0$ at fixed $\rho$,
\begin{equation}
-\frac{dW}{du}=1-\frac{2m(u)}{\sqrt u}W,
\label{eq:limitode}
\end{equation}
which is $\sigma$-free. The uncapped equation $-dW/du=1-2W/(1+u)$ has general solution
$W=(1+u)+A(1+u)^2$, so deviations from the slave $W=1+u$ contract like
$((1+u)/(1+u'))^2$ under downward integration: the band-entry boundary layer is suppressed
quadratically. Hence $\lim_{t_0\to0}V(t_0)/\sigsq=g(\rho)(1+O(\sigsq/\rho^2))$, a
\emph{relative} correction.

\section{The unconditional floor}\label{sec:U}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:U}
Assume only \eqref{eq:cap}. Then for every $t_0>0$ and every schedule,
\begin{equation}
V(t_0)\ \ge\ \sigsq\,\frac{1}{2\rho^2}(1+o(1))\ =\ \frac{1}{8\kb^2}(1+o(1))\qquad(\sigma\to0),
\label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
and the floor exceeds the true width $\sigsq$ exactly when $\rho<1/\sqrt2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(i) Comparison.} In \eqref{eq:varode} forcing and data are common and
$\partial(-dV/dt)/\partial\hat\kappa=-2V/s\le0$: smaller slope gives larger variance. So any
profile with $\hat\kappa\le\kb$ satisfies $V\ge V_{\hat\kappa\equiv\kb}$ pointwise. This is
one-sided; no lower bound on $\hat\kappa$ is used, so arbitrary overshoot is permitted.
\emph{(ii) Envelope.} Take $m\equiv\rho/2$ in \eqref{eq:limitode}; the integrating factor
$e^{-2\rho\sqrt u}$ gives $\frac{d}{du}[We^{-2\rho\sqrt u}]=-e^{-2\rho\sqrt u}$.
\emph{(iii) Integrate.} For data of sub-quadratic growth at $u=\infty$,
\[
W(0^+)=\int_0^\infty\! e^{-2\rho\sqrt u}du=2\!\int_0^\infty\! xe^{-2\rho x}dx=\frac{1}{2\rho^2},
\]
exactly, for every $\rho>0$. Undoing the rescaling gives \eqref{eq:floor};
$1/(2\rho^2)>1\iff\rho<1/\sqrt2$. It applies to the full nonlinear estimator with no
extension step, since \eqref{eq:varode} is exact.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Refinement (no overshoot).} If also $\hat\kappa\le\kappa$ (the network never
over-contracts), then $V(t_0)\ge\sigsq g(\rho)(1+o(1))$ with, for $q=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$,
$x_\mp=(1\mp q)/\rho$, $u_\mp=x_\mp^2$,
\[
W_{\mathrm{exit}}=(1+u_+)e^{-4q}+\tfrac{(3-2q)-(3+2q)e^{-4q}}{2\rho^2},\qquad
g(\rho)=1+\tfrac{W_{\mathrm{exit}}-(1+u_-)}{(1+u_-)^2}.
\]
Band edges solve $\sqrt u/(1+u)=\rho/2$, i.e.\ $\rho x^2-2x+\rho=0$ ($x=\sqrt u$), nonempty iff
$\rho<1$; above the band $W=1+u$ exactly, across it the same integrating factor with
antiderivative $-(2\rho\sqrt u+1)e^{-2\rho\sqrt u}/(2\rho^2)$ gives $W_{\mathrm{exit}}$, and
below it matching $(1+u)+A(1+u)^2$ at $u_-$ gives $g=1+A$. Here $g>1$ on $(0,1)$ and
$g=C_0/\rho^2+O(1)$ as $\rho\to0$ with $C_0=(1+3e^{-4})/2$, so the floor is
$0.1319\,\kb^{-2}$. The two constants, $1/8$ and $(1+3e^{-4})/8$, differ by $5.5\%$.

\section{Consequence, assumptions, and how to refute it}
Hold $\kb$ fixed and let $\sigma\to0$, so $J\asymp(d-k)/\sigsq\to\infty$. Then
$V/\sigsq\ge(1+o(1))/(8\kb^2\sigsq)\to\infty$: a sampler held at a fixed slope ceiling
collapses without bound relative to the data. Inverting, bounded relative error needs
$\kb\gtrsim1/\sigma$, i.e.\ a slope budget $\sim\sqrt J$. Since the bound holds for every $t_0$ and every schedule, no
annealing or truncation schedule evades it. Whether a given architecture supplies that
slope budget is a separate question, not settled here.

\emph{Assumed} (where a sceptic should push): (1) the flat-tube idealization, one normal
coordinate with Gaussian fiber decoupled from tangential position, curvature entering at
$O(s^2/\tau)$ and not carried in the constants; (2) $\hat\kappa$ is the regression slope under
the sampler's own ensemble, so a probe that measures it under $p_t$ instead estimates a
different quantity whenever $\hat\varepsilon$ is nonlinear; (3) the $o(1)$ in
\eqref{eq:floor} is the $\sigma\to0$ limit, relative correction $O(\sigsq/\rho^2)$, so the
additive gap to $g$ is $O(\sigsq/\rho^4)$.

\emph{Checks.} At \url{https://github.com/Naman84789/model-collapse-manifolds},
\texttt{falsify\_floor.py} attacks the result \emph{from} \eqref{eq:varode} (RK45 vs.\ both closed
forms; limits $\rho\to1^-$, $\rho\to0$, $\kb\to\infty$; 200 random slope profiles including
overshooting ones; boundary-layer contraction; symbolic re-derivation).
\texttt{e2e\_floor\_check.py} attacks it \emph{before} \eqref{eq:varode}: it recovers
\eqref{eq:peak} from the density, then simulates the reverse SDE directly with a slope-capped
score and measures the output variance without using \eqref{eq:varode} at all, recovering
$1/(2\rho^2)$ to $1.7\%$ at the equality case.

\emph{To refute:} exhibit a profile with $\hat\kappa\le\kb$ whose measured $V(t_0)$ falls below
$\sigsq/(2\rho^2)$.
